Hello chess enthusiasts. Two years ago I wrote three posts about my tools: [Tools and Weapons](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/tools-and-weapons), [More Tools and Weapons](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/Tools-and-Weapons-Part-2) and [Even More Tools and Weapons](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/Tools-and-Weapons-Part-3). A fourth one has been sitting half-finished since December 2024. Good thing I waited, because a lot has changed since then.

## Major updates on this site

If you have been here before, you have noticed: this site has a new look.

- **A new design.** Lighter, faster and easier to read on a phone. All posts and their addresses stayed, and the search is still there.
- **Games on real boards.** This is the change I like most. Every game on this site now plays on a board right in the post: click through the moves, jump to any position, flip the board, and it works just as well on a phone. A single position shows as a diagram, with a link to try it yourself on an analysis board. The boards are the free Lichess viewer, and it runs on this site itself, so nothing depends on an outside account any more. That was overdue: some of the old ChessBase game viewers were already broken, and I have cancelled ChessBase anyway. All of them are gone now, replaced game by game.
- **[Tournaments](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/tournaments/)** is up to date again, rebuilt from my ICCF records.
- **[Topics](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/tags/)** is cleaned up, so posts on the same subject are finally in one place.
- **[Puzzle of the Month](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/puzzles/).** From 1 December, a position from one of my own finished games each month, with the story behind it. Some I found, some my opponents found against me.
- **Game reviews on the 15th.** From 15 December, a game from my backlog each month: the tournament, the opponent, what I thought during the game and what the engine says now.

## HIARCS Chess Explorer: no regrets

In April I left ChessBase for HIARCS Chess Explorer ([Back on the Board](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/Back-on-the-Board)). Seven months later: it's amazing, and I don't regret leaving the ChessBase nonsense for a second. It does what I want. No ChatGPT annotations, just me and my own thoughts, and that's it. And I can add an external engine that isn't hooked into Ducats.

The facts: HIARCS Chess Explorer Pro runs on the Mac (Apple Silicon and Intel) and on Windows, and it comes with a [perpetual licence, no subscription](https://hiarcs.com/mac-chess-explorer-pro.html), from about $80, once.

## A game with nothing but my own notes

What does "just my own thoughts" look like? Here is a game from the Electronic Knights 2024 Preliminary (24EN02), where I shared first place, unbeaten. More about that section in March. Electronic Knights is played without engines, so the notes in the game below are exactly what I wrote while playing, nothing added.

I had Black against Chris Wainscott. My Pirc, and he chose the Austrian Attack with 4.f4. I answered with 5...c5: "I want to put a stake in the ground, meeting me on my home turf." For 11...Bxf3 and 12...Nc6 I followed Korchnoi, as my notes say: "that's how Kortschnoi played".

Afterwards I checked my notes with Stockfish. It agrees with four of them: 11...Bxf3 and 12...Nc6 were its choices too, it also preferred 21.Ne3, and 23.Ne2, "an inaccuracy" in my notes, cost White about two thirds of a pawn. White played 28.g4 instead of h4, and my plan was gone, so plan B it was; the engine says g4 was White's best move. Then 30.Qxb7+? decided the game: the engine went from about +2.6 to +4.8 for me (30.Nxd4 was better). After 31...Nf3+ it was over.

*After 30.Qxb7+?, the move that decided the game. 30...R8f7 and 31...Nf3+ followed.*

```pgn
[Event "Electronic Knights 2024 Preliminary (24EN02)"]
[White "Wainscott, Chris"]
[Black "Schroeer, Egbert"]
[Result "0-1"]

1. e4 d6 2. d4 Nf6 3. Nc3 g6 4. f4 Bg7 5. Nf3 c5
{ More aggressive and sharp tactical play. I want to put a stake in the ground, meeting me on my home turf }
6. dxc5 Qa5 7. Bd3 Qxc5 8. Qe2 O-O 9. Be3 Qa5 10. O-O Bg4 11. h3 Bxf3
{ that's how Kortschnoi played } 12. Qxf3 Nc6
{ I was not sure what the best move is here. I decided ♘c6 (Huebner - Kortschnoi) }
13. a3 Nd7 14. Bd2 Qb6+ 15. Kh1 Nc5 16. Rab1 Nxd3 17. cxd3 f5 18. Nd5
{ This position has been played many times in correspondence and classic OTB. }
18... Qb3 19. Bc3 e6 20. Bxg7 Kxg7 21. Nc3 { I think Ne3 was the better move }
21... Rf6 22. Rfc1 Raf8 23. Ne2 { I believe this is an inaccuracy } 23... e5
24. g3 fxe4 25. Qxe4 Qf7 26. fxe5 { Nxe5 or dxe5? } 26... dxe5 27. Kg1 Qe6 28.
g4 { and so my plan is gone with g4 instead of h4 and plan b is in place }
28... Qd6 29. b4 Nd4 30. Qxb7+ R8f7 31. Qe4 Nf3+ 0-1
```
