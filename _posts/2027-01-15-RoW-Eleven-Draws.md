A little entertainment before the chess. On 10 November 2026, ChessBase finally came to the Mac. Good news, you'd think. The catch: you don't buy it, you rent it. €9.95 a month, or €99.95 a year.

Then I read the feature list. "Stockfish from the cloud, without taxing your Mac." "Analyse with up to 128 cores on a remote machine." Now where have I heard that before? Right here. Stockfish on a laptop drains the battery, so I built a [remote Stockfish cluster](https://github.com/Egbert-Azure/stockfish-cluster) and sent the concept and a draft architecture to Matthias Wüllenweber himself. I never heard back ([What's next?](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/whats-next), March 2024). In November 2024, ChessBase 18 offered a remote engine with two cores, and more power for rent in Ducats ([Tools and Weapons](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/tools-and-weapons)). Now it's up to 128 cores, and they didn't even dress it up: their own Mac page describes my idea in nearly my words.

I'll take it as a compliment, a late one. I left ChessBase for HIARCS in April 2026 ([Back on the Board](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/Back-on-the-Board)), and the Mac version won't bring me back. Now, to the chess.

## The tournament

RoW Championship 2024, Semi-Final 4, ICCF. Engines allowed. Thirteen players, twelve games each, 10 moves in 50 days. It started on 30 September 2024. The strongest field I have played: eight CCMs, three IMs and one more CCE, average rating 2384. I started at 2165, the lowest in the field. [more about the format](https://chess.myvortexcloud.com/RoW-SemiFinals)

## My result

5½ out of 12: eleven draws and one loss, against CCM Pancho Amor Simplina (Philippines). Shared 11th with Johnny Owens and Eisque Nezuka. Shared first: Simon Jenkinson, Giraldo Menéndez Rodríguez and Pancho Amor Simplina.

In April I wrote: “just competing at that level and drawing against opponents like IM Allyne Smith, CCM Unes Hassim, and IM James Ellis is its own reward.”

## My opponent

Eisque Nezuka, Brazil, CCM, rated 2369. Our first game.

## The game

I had Black. Pirc Defence again, this time the 150 Attack: 1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6 4.Be3 c6 5.h3 Bg7 6.f4. For forty moves White kept the small edge the engine shows, about half a pawn (1). On move 37 he took a pawn, 37.Qxb6, and my counterplay started: 37...Qb2+ 38.Kh3 Rff7 39.Qe3 g5. By move 41 the engine showed 0.00, and it stayed there (2). From move 47 his queen went back and forth between g3 and e5 until I exchanged queens, 52...Qxe5. The draw came at move 68 (3). {% comment %}Your part: what did you see when he took on b6?{% endcomment %}

## Why it was a draw

Neither side made a mistake worth more than half a pawn. With engines on both sides, against a CCM, that is what holding looks like.
